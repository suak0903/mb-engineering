Mangdechhu HEP Tunnel.

Senior Contract Manager

Period
2013-2014
Project costs
± USD 450 million
Client
Mangdechhu Hydroelectric Project Authority, autonomous body with representatives of the Governments of India and Bhutan
Location
Trongsa, Bhutan

The project

The Rs 4,500-crore hydroelectric plant, touted as a Bhutan-India friendship project, is a 720 MW run-of-river power plant built on the Mangdechhu River in Trongsa Dzongkhag District of Central Bhutan. The project comprises a 101.5 m high concrete gravity dam from the deepest foundation level, two intake tunnels of 196 m and 150 m length, and a 13.54 km long headrace tunnel capable of discharging 118 m³ of water per second. The 152 m high surge shaft has a diameter of 13.5 m. The civil works of the project were executed under competitive bidding by contractors from India and Bhutan, splitting the project works into 3 contract packages.

Challenges

As the site is largely supplied logistically from India, the link to Indian transport infrastructure also plays an important role. Sea or air freight is usually handled via the port facilities of Calcutta, all of 1,000 km away. Freight transport within India normally takes place by road. Transport times of several weeks are customary on account of the poor efficiency and reliability of the Indian and Bhutanese transport system. As a result of the adverse logistical conditions, the contractor had to take a loss of USD 15 million in relation to the contract amount, which had to be forwarded as a claim to the client. Michael Becker prepared and negotiated this claim with success for the contractor.

My role in the project

Senior Contract Manager, responsible for:

  • Site investigation and documentation of supplementary claim
  • Preparation of supplementary claim and negotiating with the client
